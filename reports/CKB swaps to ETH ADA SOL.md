# Hash-locked Fiber swaps undercut custodial exchangers

The most efficient non-custodial route found is a bilateral swap quoted by one liquidity provider (LP), in which the CKB leg is a Fiber hold invoice locked to a SHA-256 hash (no CKB L1 transaction) and the foreign leg is the simplest native-coin hash lock that already exists on each chain. On Ethereum that lock is Boltz's EtherSwap v6 contract, reused unmodified; on Cardano it is the cardano-scaling Aiken validator with one hash builtin changed; on Solana the notes found no reusable hash lock, and the cheapest footprint measured there is a key-share address that needs no program at all. First-hand testnet runs on 12 Nov 2026 swapped **300 CKB for native ETH in 23.5 s** and ETH for CKB in 25.7 s, and costed a Cardano lock plus claim at **0.364 ADA (about $0.06)** under mainnet parameters. Nothing else can be reused for the CKB side: no solver network, bridge, stablecoin or DEX quotes native CKB without custody in November 2026. On-chain fees are under 0.1% of a $94 swap, so price is decided by the LP's spread: SimpleSwap's floating quotes sat **1.63–1.72% below mid**, against an estimated LP break-even of about **0.45%**. Relative to Nervos Talk, L1 hash-locked swaps, Fiber–Lightning swaps and hold-invoice escrow are prior art; what is new is a Fiber payment settling against native ETH, measured Cardano and CKB-VM costs, a working key-share lock, and a quantified price gap. Every result is testnet, devnet or emulator, the break-even is an estimate, and mainnet Fiber holds only 31,498 CKB of public capacity.

## No existing network quotes native CKB, so the swap must be bilateral

The obvious shortcut, plugging CKB into a network that already delivers ETH, SOL and ADA, is closed. NEAR Intents is the only large solver network with all three native assets live, but its 1Click token list has **no CKB entry**, Cardano is served only by its custodial proof-of-authority bridge, and adding a chain is a curated decision with no published permissionless procedure ([1Click token list](https://1click.chaindefuser.com/v0/tokens), [NEAR Intents bridging overview](https://docs.near-intents.org/integration/bridging/overview)). THORChain needs a node vote, a new chain client and an audit, and has no ADA either; Chainflip lists neither Cardano nor CKB ([THORChain new chain process](https://dev.thorchain.org/new-chains/new-chain-process.html), [Chainflip chains and assets](https://docs.chainflip.io/protocol/supported-chains-assets/chains-assets)). Meson has a live CKB deployment, but only for ccBTC and with no Cardano leg ([Meson relayer API](https://relayer.meson.fi/api/v1/list)). 1inch Fusion+ hard-codes a keccak256 hashlock and gates resolvers behind KYC, while Fiber invoices accept only `ckb_hash` or `sha256` ([1inch BaseEscrow.sol](https://github.com/1inch/cross-chain-swap/blob/master/contracts/BaseEscrow.sol), [1inch resolver onboarding](https://business.1inch.com/portal/assets/docs-v2/resolvers/introduction.md), [Fiber RPC README](https://github.com/nervosnetwork/fiber/blob/develop/crates/fiber-lib/src/rpc/README.md)). Garden uses SHA-256 and lists Lightning and native SOL, but settles only ERC-20s on EVM chains and admits solvers by application with a 210,000 SEED stake ([Garden API chains](https://api.garden.finance/v2/chains), [Garden solvers](https://docs.garden.finance/docs/home/fundamentals/core-concepts/solvers)).

The CKB side offers no intermediate asset to route through. UTXOSwap shut down on 11 Oct 2026, USDI was discontinued with a retail exit ending 15 Aug 2026, Force Bridge is sunset, and the Rosen Bridge integration is still pre-mainnet with open design issues ([Biweekly Update #25](https://talk.nervos.org/t/ckb-ecosystem-biweekly-update/9821/29), [USDI discontinuation thread](https://talk.nervos.org/t/usdi-on-ckb-will-be-discontinued-any-alternative-for-stablecoin/10417), [Force Bridge sunset](https://forcebridge-board.vercel.app/announcement), [Rosen DIS post 152](https://talk.nervos.org/t/dis-ckb-integration-for-rosen-bridge/9756/152)). That kills the "stablecoin shuttle" idea in the solver notes, which depended on a CKB-side DEX whose depth was never verified. A Nervos Talk post of 10 Nov 2026 states the gap plainly: there is "no live mainnet way to trade CKB against USDC, ETH, or Bitcoin without a custodian" ([Swap book thread](https://talk.nervos.org/t/swap-book-trade-ckb-for-usdc-eth-or-bitcoin-with-no-custodian-testnet/11164)).

What does exist is the primitive. Fiber v0.9.1 (11 Sep 2026) ships hold invoices, a SHA-256 hash option and multi-hop routing; upfront fees, PTLCs, LSP support and Loop In/Out are unreleased ([Fiber releases](https://github.com/nervosnetwork/fiber/releases), [Fiber README](https://github.com/nervosnetwork/fiber/blob/develop/README.md)). The Fiber team says it has "no plans to support other channels" beyond Lightning via LND, and its Cross-Chain Hub only swaps a wrapped-BTC token 1:1, so it cannot price CKB ([AMA recap](https://talk.nervos.org/t/the-fiber-network-ama-recap/10294), [cross-chain HTLC spec](https://github.com/nervosnetwork/fiber/blob/develop/docs/specs/cross-chain-htlc.md)). A cross-chain swap therefore has to be built by an LP who holds inventory on both sides and shares one secret across them.

## One SHA-256 secret links a Fiber invoice to each chain's simplest lock

**Ethereum is the finished case.** Boltz's EtherSwap v6 hashes with `sha256(preimage)`, has no owner or whitelist, and lets any third party submit a claim that pays the named recipient ([EtherSwap.sol](https://github.com/BoltzExchange/boltz-core/blob/master/contracts/EtherSwap.sol)). The research deployed the published boltz-core 5.0.0 artifact to Sepolia with no changes and drove it from two Fiber v0.9.1 testnet nodes with a script of about 150 lines. Selling CKB, the user picks the secret, pays the LP's hold invoice, the LP locks ETH for 15 blocks, the claim reveals the secret, and the LP settles the invoice: **300 CKB became 0.0001368 ETH in 23.5 s**. Buying CKB, the user's node issues an ordinary SHA-256 invoice, the user locks ETH, the LP pays, and the returned preimage lets the LP claim: 0.0002 ETH became 435.06 CKB in 25.7 s ([measured results, M1](../research_notes/CKB%20swaps%20to%20ETH%20ADA%20SOL/measured_results.md)). The claim was submitted by the LP's key and paid the user's address, but that address already held test ETH, so the strict zero-balance case was not shown.

Gas needs care. On Sepolia the lock used 135,617 gas and the claim 35,257, yet the same lock simulates at 47,417 gas against Ethereum mainnet; Sepolia appears to run a newer fork that prices state creation higher, and which change is responsible was not confirmed ([measured results, M1](../research_notes/CKB%20swaps%20to%20ETH%20ADA%20SOL/measured_results.md)). Under mainnet rules lock plus claim is about 82,000–107,000 gas, which at an effective 0.28 gwei (base fee plus tip as read on 12 Nov) and M5's ETH price of $2,006.78 is **roughly $0.05–0.06**. The Ethereum note's $0.063 was an estimate at the previous day's $2,089 price; M5 supersedes it ([Ethereum RPC reading](https://ethereum-rpc.publicnode.com), [measured results, M5](../research_notes/CKB%20swaps%20to%20ETH%20ADA%20SOL/measured_results.md)). Two risks attach to the reuse. Boltz disabled EVM swaps on 1 Aug 2026 citing an unspecified "bug in its EVM integration" and suspended all swaps two days later, and no public audit of EtherSwap was located ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/boltz-halts-swaps-as-ai-attacks-outpace-its-team/)). The contract is confirmed deployed only on Arbitrum One; Ethereum L1, Base and Optimism need a self-deployment of the MIT code ([Arbiscan EtherSwap](https://arbiscan.io/address/0xc09247f837a205bdde43960ca01bdea426f1370e)).

**Cardano needs a small edit, not a new design.** Neither public validator works as published. The cardano-scaling HTLC hashes with `blake2b_256`, which a Fiber invoice cannot match; the Cardano Foundation template uses `sha2_256` but binds no receiver and sets no claim deadline, and a test accepted a third party claiming after expiry with only the secret ([cardano-scaling/htlc](https://github.com/cardano-scaling/htlc), [Cardano Foundation template](https://github.com/cardano-foundation/cardano-template-and-ecosystem-monitoring/tree/main/htlc), [measured results, M4](../research_notes/CKB%20swaps%20to%20ETH%20ADA%20SOL/measured_results.md)). Swapping the hash builtin in the first and adding a 32-byte secret check gives a usable PlutusV3 validator. With mainnet epoch-667 parameters the lock costs 0.172189 ADA and the claim 0.192067 ADA using a reference script, **0.364256 ADA in total**, with at least 1.349030 ADA in the locked output and a one-off 0.198721 ADA plus a recoverable 3.947960 ADA deposit to publish the script. The Cardano note's 0.4–0.5 ADA per swap and 1.4–1.6 ADA minimum output were estimates; the measured values replace them. These figures come from an emulator, with execution units confirmed by a real cardano-node through Koios preprod; nothing was submitted to a live Cardano network and no Fiber-linked ADA swap was run. One real friction appeared: a receiver with zero ADA cannot claim alone, because the claim needs collateral of about 0.29 ADA and the receiver's signature, so a sponsor must co-sign, an interactive round that EtherSwap's submit-by-anyone claim avoids. Cardano's channel projects do not help: Konduit productises only the ADA-to-Lightning direction and reports no commercial gateway, and a Hydra head has a fixed participant set, so it cannot pay an arbitrary L1 address without an extra L1 step ([Konduit close-out report](https://github.com/cardano-lightning/blog/blob/main/pages/blog/closeout-report.md), [IOG on scaling with Hydra](https://www.iog.io/news/scaling-cardano-applications-with-hydra)).

**Solana is the least covered leg.** The notes contain no survey of reusable Solana hash-lock programs, which is a gap in this research rather than a finding. The project's own result logs, which sit outside the notes folder, record a purpose-built SHA-256 escrow program on Solana devnet settling 500 CKB for 0.005601632 SOL in 4.4 s against a Fiber testnet payment on 11 Nov 2026 ([project log](../results/fiber-testnet-ckb2sol.json), [Solana claim transaction](https://explorer.solana.com/tx/3s3NY6Mc6LgT1e1qTYgJSSQhUnYZ57Bf6BTh4JE3HeqYE7YFJhD1ZpNL8JcdhsXv1P4YjhAgTtxox4sAr4kC1H5M?cluster=devnet)). That is new code, not reused infrastructure.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| ETH | EtherSwap v6 (boltz-core 5.0.0) | none | 170,874 gas on Sepolia; 82k–107k gas simulated on mainnet (about $0.05–0.06) | 23.5 s and 25.7 s, testnet |
| ADA | cardano-scaling/htlc | `blake2b_256` to `sha2_256`, 32-byte secret check | 0.364256 ADA (about $0.06) | not run; costed in emulator |

## Spread, not gas, decides whether the swap beats SimpleSwap

At 100,000 CKB, worth $93.95 on 12 Nov 2026, the Ethereum and Cardano settlement fees are each about six cents, under 0.1% of the trade. The live benchmark is far wider. SimpleSwap's floating quotes at 19:42 UTC were **1.72% below mid for ETH, 1.69% for ADA and 1.63% for SOL**, between $1.53 and $1.62 on that trade ([measured results, M5](../research_notes/CKB%20swaps%20to%20ETH%20ADA%20SOL/measured_results.md)). The solver note's guess of 0.5–1% for instant exchangers was an estimate from prior knowledge; the measured figure is higher. For scale, NEAR Intents dry-run quotes between the three majors came in 0.21–0.60% below mid including its 0.25% keyless API fee, which the same note had only inferred from the fee schedule.

An LP's cost is mostly the CKB hedge. Binance's CKB/USDT book showed a 0.21% spread, $4,756 of bids within 0.5% of mid and $219,444 of 24-hour volume; OKX and Bybit returned no CKB spot instrument. Selling $1,000 of CKB lands 0.145% below mid, and $10,000 lands 0.473% below. From those readings the research derives a break-even of **about 0.45% in $1,000 clips, 0.75% in $10,000 clips, and 0.1% when flow nets out**, including 0.08% of option value for a three-minute window. This is an estimate built from the readings, not a measurement, and Binance's taker fee was not re-read that day. It still leaves more than a percentage point of room under SimpleSwap at small sizes, and the room shrinks quickly with size because the only hedging venue is thin.

The option window is the design variable that matters. A hash-locked swap hands the secret holder a free option whose value grows with volatility and the square root of time; Han, Lin and Yu price it at 2–3% of notional for cryptocurrencies at their default timelocks ([ePrint 2019/896](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/896)). The 15-block Ethereum lock keeps the LP's exposure to about three minutes. The user's exposure is longer: the Fiber payment as configured expires after 6 h 40 min, so the reverse direction required the user to lock ETH for about seven hours, and a stalled counterparty can tie funds up for that long ([measured results, M1](../research_notes/CKB%20swaps%20to%20ETH%20ADA%20SOL/measured_results.md)). Fiber ships rate limits but no upfront or hold fee, so this risk is priced by reputation, not protocol ([AMA recap](https://talk.nervos.org/t/the-fiber-network-ama-recap/10294)).

Capacity is the hard ceiling. Public mainnet Fiber had **40 nodes, 19 channels and 31,498 CKB** of total capacity on 12 Nov 2026, with a median channel of 749 CKB ([Fiber dashboard API](https://api-dashboard.fiber.channel/analysis_hourly?net=mainnet)). That is about $30 at M5's price; the Fiber note's "$38" was an estimate from a 1 Nov forum price. The 100,000 CKB trade used for the SimpleSwap comparison is three times the whole public graph, so an LP must fund a direct channel to each taker. By this report's own reckoning, a one-off swap from L1 CKB then spends two L1 transactions opening and closing that channel, the same count an L1 lock needs, which is where the next design earns its place.

## Key-share locks delete the foreign contract but save only cents

The alternative measured here moves all logic to CKB. A lock script on CKB L1 releases to the maker when the maker reveals a key share; the foreign coins sit at an ordinary address whose private key is the sum of both parties' shares, so the foreign chain sees two plain transfers and no contract. The layout follows the Bitcoin–Monero swap, which needs a cross-curve proof because Bitcoin cannot check an Ed25519 scalar; CKB-VM can check it directly ([arXiv 2101.12332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12332)). The research built that lock in about 320 lines of Rust and measured it in CKB-VM: **2,232,165 cycles for an Ed25519 claim and 2,442,807 for secp256k1**, in transactions of 482–548 bytes, with a 61 CKB cell ([measured results, M3](../research_notes/CKB%20swaps%20to%20ETH%20ADA%20SOL/measured_results.md)). That is under 4% of the default 70,000,000-cycle relay limit ([ckb.toml](https://github.com/nervosnetwork/ckb/blob/develop/resource/ckb.toml)). Joint addresses were funded and swept on Solana devnet, Sepolia and a Cardano emulator, and neither share alone could sign ([measured results, M2](../research_notes/CKB%20swaps%20to%20ETH%20ADA%20SOL/measured_results.md)).

The savings are smaller than the cryptography note predicted. That note called a plain 21,000-gas transfer "a real saving" over two contract calls on Ethereum; this was an estimate. Measured, two transfers cost 42,000 gas under mainnet rules against 82,000–107,000 for EtherSwap, but 225,600 against 170,874 under Sepolia's rules, so the design is not reliably cheaper on Ethereum. On Cardano the two transfers cost 0.332542 ADA against 0.364256 ADA, a saving of about half a US cent. The real gains are structural: nothing to deploy or audit on the foreign chain, and no collateral on Cardano. The costs are structural too: the CKB side is an L1 cell, not a Fiber payment; the refunding party must come online or be punished; and the end-to-end swap was not shown, only its two halves. A first version also let anyone rebuild a broadcast claim and burn the capacity as fee, fixed with a 0.001 CKB fee cap.

Proof-of-payment, where CKB verifies a foreign transfer through a light client, is not practical yet. The verifiers available for CKB-VM cost about 63 million cycles (SP1 PLONK), 96–121 million (Groth16) and 134–146 million (Mithril certificate), so only the first fits the default relay limit, and none is deployed ([SP1 verifier thread](https://talk.nervos.org/t/optimized-sp1-verifier-for-ckb-vm/10144), [ZK research notes](https://talk.nervos.org/t/research-notes-what-zero-knowledge-proofs-enable-on-ckb/10368), [Chiral open-source post](https://talk.nervos.org/t/chiral-is-now-open-source-a-trust-minimized-cardano-and-nervos-ckb-port-for-you-to-review-break-and-test/10421)). The cryptography note's reading that verification is "not the bottleneck" compared against the block limit, not the relay limit. Ethereum finality adds 12–15 minutes, Mithril certifies transactions about 100 blocks behind the tip, and the notes found no deployed Solana light client ([Across V4](https://docs.across.to/guides/concepts/across-v4), [Mithril transaction certification](https://mithril.network/doc/mithril/advanced/mithril-certification/cardano-transactions)).

## What this adds to the Nervos Talk record

Several pieces are already on the forum and should be credited as prior art. Swap book (10 Nov 2026, testnet) does L1 hash-locked swaps of CKB against USDC and ETH on Base Sepolia and BTC on testnet3 in about three minutes, with a bot quoting about 2% off market; it does not use Fiber. Twine uses Fiber hold invoices as escrow for fiat trades. The Cross-Chain Hub and at least four demos swap between Fiber and Bitcoin Lightning on one payment hash. Perun runs CKB–Ethereum channel swaps on testnet. Chiral has Cardano and CKB verifying each other through light clients, and a March 2025 concept post proposed SPV-verified swaps without building them ([Swap book](https://talk.nervos.org/t/swap-book-trade-ckb-for-usdc-eth-or-bitcoin-with-no-custodian-testnet/11164), [Twine](https://talk.nervos.org/t/twine-buy-and-sell-ckb-without-depositing-on-an-exchange-fiber-p2p-poc/10741), [Perun AMA recap](https://talk.nervos.org/t/perun-channels-ama-questions-open/10128), [CKB as a Gateway of Cross-Chain DEX](https://talk.nervos.org/t/ckb-as-a-gateway-of-cross-chain-dex/8673)).

The forum search found no swap in which the CKB leg is a Fiber channel payment and the other leg is a lock on Ethereum, Cardano or Solana, no Fiber-plus-Boltz work, and no CKB lock that reveals a key share ([Nervos Talk search](https://talk.nervos.org/search?q=fiber%20atomic%20swap)). X, Discord and Telegram were not searchable, so the claim covers the forum and the Fiber repository only. Against that record, five things are new. A Fiber hold invoice settled against native ETH through an unmodified, already-published contract, in both directions, in under 26 seconds. The Cardano leg has measured mainnet-parameter costs and a documented reason why neither public validator can be used as published. A key-share lock exists for CKB with measured cycle counts for both curves, plus the negative result that it does not reliably save gas on Ethereum. The price case is quantified against a named custodial competitor on the same minute's market. And Sepolia's gas no longer predicts mainnet for this contract.

## Conclusion

The design question turned out to be the small one. Hash lock, key share or proof changes the bill by cents on a $94 trade, while the choice of who quotes, how they hedge and how long anyone holds a free option moves it by a percentage point or more. The scarce resource is a market maker willing to hold CKB inventory against a single thin order book, and Fiber's contribution is to shrink that maker's risk window to seconds on the CKB side at no on-chain cost.

That reframes the next steps. A mainnet pilot is bounded by channel capacity and Fiber's own maturity before it is bounded by cryptography, so small sizes and direct channels come first. The unproven items are specific: an ETH claim to a zero-balance address, a live Cardano submission tied to a Fiber payment, a reusable Solana hash lock, an end-to-end key-share swap with consensus-level timelocks, and an audit or minimal fork of EtherSwap given Boltz's undisclosed bug. Shortening the Fiber expiry that forces a seven-hour ETH lock would do more for users than any further fee optimisation.
